- **FM-221** [BROAD] An adversarial review fan-out typically includes a suite-integrity lens whose entire method is to mutate the implementation and observe which assertions fail -- that is what makes it worth running. Point that lens and its reading siblings at one checkout and the conflict is structural, not incidental: every suite result any lens produces during the window is untrustworthy, and untrustworthy in BOTH directions. Only one direction announces itself. A reader sampling while the mutant is installed sees a red it may file as a finding; a reader sampling during the mutating lens's RESTORE window sees a clean green and concludes more than it should, with nothing visibly wrong. The orchestrator cannot detect either case after the fact, because the tree is clean by the time results are collected. Two mitigations, both required: give every mutating agent its own checkout (git worktree add per lens, or git archive into scratch for read-only lenses), and require every agent that runs a suite to report the git status of the tree it ran against ALONGSIDE the result, so attribution does not depend on the agent being conscientious enough to check. Serializing the lenses also works and is the wrong trade -- it discards the parallelism that makes a fan-out worth running, and does not help when two unrelated campaigns touch one repo
